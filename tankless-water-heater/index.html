A tankless heater warms water as it passes through, so it has no tank to leak or run out. That convenience comes with a catch. The heat exchanger is sensitive to mineral scale, and the gas, vent and electrical requirements are stricter than for a standard tank.

We help homeowners decide whether tankless fits their house, and we service units that already hang on the wall.

Is tankless a good fit for your home?

Tankless suits households that want continuous hot water and wall space instead of a floor tank. It struggles when several fixtures run at once and the unit is too small, so sizing matters more than brand.

Converting from a tank usually means a bigger gas line, new venting through the wall or roof and sometimes an electrical outlet. We look at each item on a site visit before quoting.

Why scale builds up and what flushing does

Plantation Utilities treats groundwater from the Biscayne aquifer with membrane softening, but the city's own water quality report does not list a hardness figure. Testing your tap tells you what your home actually receives. Scale forms when minerals bake onto the heat exchanger, and it cuts output and triggers error codes.

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit, then rinses it clean. We recommend doing this on a regular schedule, and homes with a softener or filter ahead of the heater may stretch it out. See water softener and filtration if you want to treat the water itself.

Repairs we handle

  • Ignition and flame sensor faults that cause shutdowns.
  • Error codes tied to venting, flow or temperature sensors.
  • Clogged inlet filters and mineral-blocked heat exchangers.
  • Condensate drain problems on high-efficiency models.
  • Gas supply and pressure issues that starve the burner.

Installing a new tankless heater

The permit comes first. Plantation requires one for water heater work, and the city's same-day program covers qualifying single-family homes. We handle that step. If your current tank has failed, read our page on water heater replacement to compare timing, because a tankless conversion takes longer than a like-for-like tank swap.

Choosing between repair and replacement

A tankless unit that is a few years old and has a failed sensor or igniter is usually worth fixing. A unit with a cracked heat exchanger or repeated failures may not be. We show you the error history and give an honest recommendation, so you are not paying for a repair that will not last.

Sizing a tankless heater

Add up the flow of fixtures that run at the same time. A shower might use two to three gallons per minute, a kitchen tap about one and a half, and a dishwasher or washer a little more. The unit must meet that flow at the temperature rise you need. Groundwater in South Florida enters the house warm compared with northern states, so the temperature rise is smaller, which helps capacity.

Codes and permits for this work

Plumbing in Florida is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). In practice, gas sizing, venting and the condensate drain on a tankless unit are set by code and by the manufacturer's instructions. If you want to know what applies to your address, ask the dispatcher and we explain it before the visit.

What affects tankless water heater cost

Installed cost depends far more on the house than on the unit.

Gas line and venting
Larger gas pipe and a new vent route are the biggest add-ons.
Unit capacity
Bigger flow rates mean larger, costlier models.
Service history
A unit never flushed may need a longer descaling visit.
Water quality
Hard water shortens intervals between flushes.
