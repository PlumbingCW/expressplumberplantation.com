Plantation Midtown is a mixed-use district in south-central Plantation. The Plantation Midtown Development District covers about 860 acres and was created by the city in 1988 as the Downtown West Development District.

It was renamed the Central Plantation Development District in 1988 and Plantation Midtown in 2003. This page is for commercial and condo plumbing in that district and is not about a single residential subdivision.

What the district contains

A state review of the district used county property data to count its uses. It found roughly 69 office buildings, 38 retail properties, 15 restaurants and 5 financial institutions. It also counted 328 condominiums, 155 single-family homes and 64 small multifamily buildings.

Those numbers are approximate and come from a report, not a current survey. They show that the area serves both businesses and residents.

Condo buildings and shared systems

In a condo building, a leak may start in a neighbor's unit or in a shared riser. Your association and the owner of the unit above may both be involved, so document what you see.

We repair plumbing inside your unit and can describe what we find to your association. We cannot decide who pays, and we do not claim to.

Plumbing services we offer in the district

Common requests include drain cleaning for slow lines, hydro jetting for heavy buildup and water heater repair for offices and restaurants.

A building with repeat backups may need a sewer camera inspection to see what the line is doing. We explain findings before recommending any repair.

What affects the scope of a commercial job

The size of a job depends on the building, the fixture count and how many tenants share a line. Access hours matter too, because a restaurant or shop may need work before opening or after closing.

Photos of the problem, a floor plan or a note about past backups help us plan the visit. We explain the scope and the price before work starts.

Before we arrive: what to do while you wait

Find the nearest shutoff

In a tenant space, locate the fixture valve and ask the property manager where the main or riser valve is.

Stop using affected fixtures

Close restrooms or kitchen sinks that back up and put up a wet floor sign.

Notify the manager

Tell the landlord or association so shared lines and neighbors can be checked.

Document the damage

Photograph standing water and stains before cleanup.
