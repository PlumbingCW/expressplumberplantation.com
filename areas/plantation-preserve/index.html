Plantation Preserve is a public golf course and park at 7050 West Broward Boulevard. The City of Plantation's planning document places the Preserve and two lakes in the northwest part of the Peters Road Neighborhood.

That quadrant is reached from Dorado Parkway off Broward Boulevard and from SW 69th Avenue off Peters Road. The city describes the neighborhood as mostly well-maintained single-family homes.

The land and the neighborhood

The Preserve land was once part of Frederick Peters' 10,000-acre tract. Peters donated 300 acres for the first Plantation Golf Club in 1950, and the club closed in 1998. The city bought the course in 2001 and restored it as a public park inside a golf course.

The city's history page does not say when the surrounding homes were built. We do not guess a date or a pipe type for them.

Plumbing notes for homes near lakes and open space

This is general plumbing information. Homes by lakes and open land often have large lawns, irrigation lines and outdoor taps. Those pieces add joints and valves that can crack, loosen or wear out.

A sudden jump in a water bill or a soggy patch near the street may point to a leak in a supply or irrigation line. A meter test is a simple first check, and our leak detection work follows up when it shows movement.

Plumbing services nearby homes ask about

Common calls include toilet repair for running or clogged toilets, faucet repair for drips and drain cleaning when a sink or tub slows.

Water heater problems also come up, and repair is the first step before a replacement is considered. Our plumbers explain what they find before work starts.

A note on nearby communities

A neighborhood profile for Plantation Preserve mentions adjoining streets and communities such as Hawthorne Village, a 55 and older community next to the course. We do not treat those as part of this page.

If you live in an age-restricted or association-managed community, ask about its rules for vendors and shared lines before a visit.

Questions to ask your association or landlord

Before you call, find out who maintains the shared lines and who handles water shutoffs after hours. Ask where the riser valves are and whether a contact number exists for emergencies.

If you rent, tell the owner before you book repairs. Many leases require notice, and the owner may have a preferred plumber.
