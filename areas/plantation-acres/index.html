Plantation Acres sits in the west of Plantation and is one of the city's best-known large-lot areas. The Plantation Acres Improvement District describes it as running between Hiatus Road and Flamingo Road, and between the North New River Canal and NW 28th Court.

Most of the district is single-family estates on one acre or more, with some smaller lots, multifamily buildings, schools, parks and shops mixed in. Horse farms and pastures are part of the local character.

Where Plantation Acres sits

The improvement district says it lies primarily within the City of Plantation, which means a few edges may fall in a neighboring town. If you are unsure which city your address belongs to, check your property record before you book work.

Sunrise Boulevard and Broward Boulevard both pass through the district. Our Sunrise page covers the town on the other side of Sunrise Boulevard if your address sits near the border.

Plantation Acres at a glance

Drainage district

The area began in 1963 as the Dixie Drainage District and was renamed the Plantation Acres Improvement District in 1982.

Lot sizes

The district describes most of its land as one acre or larger single-family estates.

Horse country

Horse farms, grazing pastures and a regional saddle club are part of the district's rural character.

Mixed uses

Schools, churches, parks, shops and multifamily buildings sit among the estates.

What a big lot means for plumbing

The facts below are general plumbing information, not claims about any one street. A larger lot usually means a longer pipe between the meter and the house, and a longer pipe has more places to leak. A wet patch in the yard, a meter that spins when everything is off or a lawn that stays soggy are all worth a call.

Outbuildings, barns and guest houses often have their own taps and water lines. Hose bibs and irrigation lines add more joints that can crack or loosen. Our leak detection work traces these lines before anyone digs.

Water and sewer service at your address

The district's own pages focus on drainage, canals and pump stations. They do not say whether a given home uses city water and sewer, a well or a septic tank. Utility service can differ from one address to the next.

Ask the city or check your records so you know what serves your house. Tell us when you call and we plan the visit around it. We do not assume a system type without seeing it.

Plumbing services Plantation Acres homes ask about

Common requests include drain cleaning for slow sinks and tubs, water heater repair when hot water fades and leak detection for wet spots and high bills. Larger homes may also need a water line repair between the meter and the house.

If a heater is old or keeps failing, we can talk through replacement. A plumber looks at the unit and the space before suggesting a swap.

Before we arrive: what to do while you wait

Find the main shutoff

On a large lot the valve may sit near the meter at the road. Turn it clockwise to stop the water.

Switch off power near water

Cut the breaker to any wet room or outbuilding. Do not stand in water to reach a panel.

Note where the water shows

Take photos of wet ground, stains or a spinning meter. They help us find the source faster.

Tell us about gates and access

Give gate codes, animal details and the route to the house when you call.
