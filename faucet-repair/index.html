Most faucet trouble comes from worn internal parts, such as cartridges, washers and O-rings. Sink leaks come from the supply lines, the drain connections or a failing seal. All of these are fixable, and many are quick.

If water is escaping under the sink, close the small valves on the supply lines first.

Water under the sink right now

Turn the shutoff valves under the sink clockwise. If there are none or they will not close, close the main water valve. Put a bucket under the leak and empty the cabinet, since wood swells quickly.

Call us. We trace whether the water comes from the faucet, a supply line or the drain.

Fixing a drip, spray or loose handle

A steady drip usually means a worn cartridge or washer. A faucet that sprays from the base has a failed O-ring or body seal. A loose handle may only need tightening, but corrosion inside can mean replacement. We stock common parts and tell you honestly when a new faucet makes more sense than another repair.

Sink drains and traps

Leaks at the P-trap, a slow drain or a bad smell often trace back to loose slip nuts, cracked pipe or a worn washer. Persistent clogs may need drain cleaning. A kitchen sink with a disposal has more joints, and our garbage disposal repair page covers that side.

Replacing a faucet

We remove the old faucet and install the new one, with new supply lines and a leak test. If you bring your own faucet, we mount it. If you want advice, we explain the difference between ceramic cartridge and compression styles and which ones tend to last. We also repair shower valves and tub spouts.

Choosing a replacement faucet

Match the number of holes in the sink. Choose a finish that wipes clean. Look at the valve type, because ceramic disc cartridges tend to outlast older washers. A pull-down spout suits a kitchen, and a single-handle model suits a small bathroom.

If you bring the faucet, we mount it. If you want advice, we explain options without pushing a brand.

Showers, tubs and outdoor spigots

The same worn parts that make kitchen faucets drip also cause trouble in showers and tubs. A shower that will not stay hot or cold may have a failed mixing cartridge. A tub spout that leaks when the shower runs has a bad diverter.

Outdoor spigots can drip inside the wall if a vacuum breaker fails or a washer wears. We repair or replace them and check for any dampness behind the wall.

A quick tip for any faucet: close the small stop valve under the sink before you start any repair. If the valve itself leaks or will not turn, tell us when you call so the plumber brings a replacement.

Codes and permits for this work

Plumbing in Florida is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). In practice, replacement faucets and supply lines must be listed for potable water use. If you want to know what applies to your address, ask the dispatcher and we explain it before the visit.
