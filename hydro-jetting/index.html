Hydro jetting uses a hose with a rotating nozzle that sends high-pressure water in all directions inside the pipe. The water cuts through grease, scrapes scale from walls and shreds soft root growth. The result is a clean bore rather than a narrow tunnel.

It is the right answer for restaurants, multifamily buildings and any home whose main line backs up repeatedly.

When a main line backs up, start here

Water in a shower when the toilet flushes, a floor drain that bubbles, or sewage rising in the lowest fixture all say the main is blocked. Stop using water and call. Every flush adds to the backup.

We dispatch day or night, find the cleanout, and decide whether a cable or a jetter is the right tool.

Jetting compared with cabling

Jetting is not always safe for old, cracked or collapsed pipe. We look inside with a camera first when the line is old or has a history of trouble, which is why sewer camera inspection often pairs with this service.

Grease, roots and scale in Plantation lines

Commercial kitchens anywhere in Plantation produce grease that coats pipes every day. Residential lines pick up roots where trees sit near older clay or cast iron laterals. Scale from minerals narrows pipes slowly and shows up as drains that never quite run at full speed.

For businesses, ongoing jetting schedules are worth discussing, and our commercial plumbing page explains what we offer.

What to expect during a jetting visit

  1. We locate or install access at a cleanout so the hose can go in cleanly.
  2. We run a camera when the pipe condition is uncertain.
  3. We jet the line section by section and recover debris at the cleanout.
  4. We camera again if needed and explain what we found.

Why the cleanout matters

A clean access point lets the jetter hose enter the line without disturbing the house. If your home has no outdoor cleanout, we can usually install one. It pays for itself, because every future cleaning is faster and cleaner.

Maintenance jetting for restaurants and buildings

A schedule prevents emergencies. A kitchen that produces grease every day may need jetting several times a year. A small office may need it far less often. We look at the line, ask about use and recommend an interval, then record the date of each visit.

Skipping maintenance does not save money in the long run. A backup during service hours costs a day of sales and a cleanup bill.

Codes and permits for this work

Plumbing in Florida is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). In practice, jetting does not need a permit, but any pipe we find broken must be repaired to code. If you want to know what applies to your address, ask the dispatcher and we explain it before the visit.

What to do before we arrive

  • Stop running water into the drain, including dishwashers and washing machines.
  • Do not pour chemical drain openers into the line, because they put the crew at risk.
  • Clear the area around the cleanout and any floor drains.
  • Keep children and pets away from standing wastewater.
