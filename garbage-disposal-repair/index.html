Disposals fail in a few common ways. They jam, they trip the reset, they leak at the seals or they clog the drain below. We diagnose which one it is and repair or replace the unit.

Keep your hand out of the drain. Never reach into a disposal, even when it is off.

Kitchen sink backed up or leaking

Turn off the disposal and the power switch. If water is collecting under the sink, close the supply valves and empty the cabinet. If the sink is full of dirty water, stop running the dishwasher, because it drains into the same line.

Then call us. A disposal problem often has a drain clog behind it.

A disposal that hums or will not run

A humming unit with no movement is usually jammed. A silent unit may have tripped its reset button or lost power. We free the jam with the proper hex tool at the bottom, check the reset and look for a failed motor. If the motor has burned out, replacement is the sensible route.

Leaks and bad smells

Leaks at the top flange, the dishwasher connection or the unit body each have different causes. A leaking body means the housing has cracked, and the unit must go. Smells from food trapped under the baffle clean up quickly, while smells from the trap or drain line may need drain cleaning.

Replacing a disposal

We remove the old unit, set the new mount, connect the drain and dishwasher line and test for leaks. Horsepower and feed type matter. A heavy household cooking benefits from more power. If the sink also needs attention, our faucet and sink repair page covers it.

Using a disposal without jams

Run cold water before, during and after grinding. Feed scraps gradually, not all at once. Skip stringy vegetables such as celery and corn husks, and keep grease, bones and large pits out. Citrus peels freshen the unit, but they do not clean the drain.

If the disposal hums and does not turn, shut it off and call, or use the hex socket at the bottom to free the jam once the power is off.

Look at noise insulation as well. Better insulated models run quieter, which matters in an open kitchen.

Dishwasher and disposal connections

The dishwasher drain hose usually ties into the disposal inlet or the sink drain. If the dishwasher leaves dirty water in the bottom, the knockout plug may not have been removed, the hose may be kinked or the line may need an air gap or a high loop. We check the full path from dishwasher to trap, because a disposal problem and a dishwasher problem often share a cause.

A clean fix also protects your cabinet floor, where a slow leak can rot particleboard before anyone notices.

Codes and permits for this work

Plumbing in Florida is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). In practice, disposal installs involve a trap and a drain connection that the code governs, plus electrical work to the National Electrical Code (NEC). If you want to know what applies to your address, ask the dispatcher and we explain it before the visit.
