Sewer line cleaning targets the large pipe running from your house to the city connection. It is a bigger job than a sink clog, and it often needs a long cable or a jetter that can travel many feet.

Look for slow drains in several rooms, a gurgle in the toilet when you run the shower and water backing up in the lowest fixture.

Where the water goes when the main is blocked

Wastewater follows the lowest opening. In a single-story home, that is a shower drain or a floor drain. Every flush after that pushes more waste toward the same spot.

Turn off the washing machine, stop flushing and call. Mop up with gloves and keep the area ventilated, then wait for us.

Our process, from cleanout to clear flow

  1. We find the cleanout, often a capped pipe outside the house near the wall.
  2. We open it, check for pressure and send a cable machine into the line.
  3. We cut through the obstruction, then flush with water to confirm flow.
  4. If the line has heavy buildup, we recommend hydro jetting to finish the cleaning.

Common causes in Broward neighborhoods

Tree roots find their way into older lateral lines through joints. Grease from kitchens builds a hard layer over time. Flushed wipes and paper products catch on rough pipe walls. In larger buildings, years of buildup combine into a stubborn blockage.

If the same blockage returns, a camera inspection shows whether the cause is the pipe itself.

No cleanout? We can usually create access

Some older homes have no outdoor cleanout. We can pull a toilet or open a vent to reach the line, and we may recommend installing a proper cleanout so future service is faster and cleaner.

Cleaning versus repairing

Cleaning removes what is in the pipe. It does not fix a pipe that is broken. If the line is cracked or sagging, the clog returns. That is why we pair a cleaning with a quick camera look when the blockage seems stubborn, and why our sewer line repair crews are part of the same team.

If the camera shows a healthy pipe, a cleaning is all you need, and we say so.

Warning signs before a full blockage

  • A slow drain that clears after a few minutes and then slows again.
  • A faint sewer smell near the floor drain or outside the cleanout.
  • Bubbles in a toilet when you run the washing machine.
  • A wet or unusually green strip of lawn over the line.
  • A toilet that needs two flushes in a house that used to need one.

Codes and permits for this work

Plumbing in Florida is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). In practice, cleaning a main is routine work, and any new cleanout we add must meet code. If you want to know what applies to your address, ask the dispatcher and we explain it before the visit.
