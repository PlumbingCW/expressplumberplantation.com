New plumbing needs more than connected pipes. It needs correct slopes, venting, shutoffs and a permit trail. We do the work in a way that satisfies the inspector and keeps working for years.

We work with homeowners, landlords and general contractors on projects of many sizes.

What we install

  • Sinks, toilets, tubs and showers, including valves and drains.
  • Washing machine boxes, dishwasher lines and ice maker supplies.
  • Outdoor hose bibs, irrigation connections and utility sinks.
  • New water and drain lines for additions and conversions.
  • Shutoff valves, pressure regulators and filtration connections.

Planning around the house

Slab-on-grade homes make drain changes more involved because the concrete has to be opened. We discuss options before you commit, such as above-slab solutions or relocating the fixture. For a second-floor addition, we plan routes through walls and chases.

Code and permits

Florida follows the Florida Building Code Plumbing, which draws on the International Plumbing Code, and Plantation reviews permits for plumbing work. We pull permits for work that requires them, schedule inspections and correct any issue found. Our water heater installation page covers a specific permit path.

Working with your contractor or on your own

Rough-in work happens before walls close, and trim-out comes after. We coordinate timing so finishes are not delayed. If you are living in the house, we keep water available whenever possible. For leaks or failures during a project, we can also help through leak detection.

Phases of an installation project

Rough-in comes first, with supply and drain lines placed before walls close. An inspection follows. After finishes go in, trim-out connects fixtures, tests them and closes the job. Each phase has its own timing, so we coordinate with other trades.

Tell us your schedule early. If tile and cabinets have long lead times, we plan the plumbing around them.

Common mistakes we fix

  • Fixtures set without proper venting, which causes gurgling and slow drains.
  • Supply lines without shutoff valves.
  • Drain slopes that are too flat or too steep.
  • Missing access panels for tub valves and traps.
  • Water heaters moved without updating relief lines.

Getting a fair, comparable price

Describe the project in the same way to every plumber. List the fixtures, say who supplies them, mention the floor and wall conditions and state when you want the work done. Ask whether permits and inspections are included. Prices from different contractors are comparable only when the scope matches.

We put the scope in writing so you can see exactly what the price covers.

Appliances that need plumbing

  • Dishwashers, with a supply line, drain hose and high loop or air gap.
  • Washing machines, with a recessed box, valves and a standpipe.
  • Refrigerator ice makers and water dispensers.
  • Outdoor kitchens and wet bars with hot and cold supply.
  • Utility sinks in garages and laundry rooms.

Tell us the model if you already bought the appliance. Specifications decide the connection type and the clearances.

Codes and permits for this work

Plumbing in Florida is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). In practice, new plumbing needs a permit and an inspection by the building department. If you want to know what applies to your address, ask the dispatcher and we explain it before the visit.
