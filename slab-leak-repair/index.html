A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs beneath or inside the concrete foundation. Because most Plantation homes sit on slabs rather than crawlspaces, the pipes are out of sight and out of reach, and a pinhole can run for weeks before anyone notices.

The damage includes soaked flooring, a rising water bill and, in the worst case, soil washed out from under the foundation.

Warning signs under the floor

  • A warm or hot patch on tile or wood that has no heating source nearby.
  • Water running sounds when every fixture is off.
  • A water meter that keeps moving with all taps closed.
  • Damp carpet, buckled flooring or cracks in tile grout.
  • Low water pressure or a hot water bill that suddenly climbs.

First steps when you suspect a slab leak

Shut the main water valve and see whether the problem stops. If the water bill or meter has been climbing, take a photo of the reading before and after. Keep the main off if floors are wet.

Then call us. Because the leak sits under concrete, the longer it runs, the more it saturates the soil and the harder the repair.

Locating the leak without tearing up the house

We use listening gear, pressure testing and thermal imaging to narrow the leak to a small area. Our leak detection page describes the process. Once we know the line and the spot, we can choose how to fix it.

That precision keeps the opening small, which protects flooring and cuts repair cost.

Three ways to fix a slab leak

Break the slab and repair the pipe

We open a small section of concrete directly above the leak, repair the pipe and restore the slab. It is direct and works well when only one spot is bad.

Reroute the line

We abandon the leaking line under the slab and run new pipe through walls or the attic. It avoids breaking more concrete and is a common option when the original pipe is in bad shape.

Repipe the home

If pipes in the house are failing in several places, repiping may be the better long-term answer than chasing leaks one at a time.

Protecting your home during the repair

We cover floors, set up dust control and keep the work area contained. Concrete cutting creates noise and dust, so we plan around your household and tell you the schedule. Pets and children should stay away from the work area.

After the repair, the slab is patched and the pipes tested. Flooring replacement is usually a separate trade, and we can tell you what to expect.

Keeping a record for your insurer

Insurance policies treat slab leaks in different ways. Keep photos of the damaged area, the meter reading, the water bill and our written findings. Our description of the leak location and the repair gives the adjuster what they need. If your policy has a deductible or an exclusion, ask before work starts so there are no surprises.

Codes and permits for this work

Plumbing in Florida is governed by the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). In practice, rerouting or replacing a line under or through a slab is done to code and may need a permit. If you want to know what applies to your address, ask the dispatcher and we explain it before the visit.

What affects slab leak repair cost

Slab leaks vary enormously because of how the plumbing runs under your house.

Depth and location of the leak
A leak in an open room is easier than one under a built-in cabinet or tile.
Repair method
Spot repair, rerouting and repiping each carry different labor and materials.
Floor finishes
Tile, hardwood and carpet affect how much restoration follows.
Number of leaks
Multiple failures raise the case for rerouting or repiping.
