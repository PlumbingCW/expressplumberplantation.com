Plantation homes and the pipes inside them

Plantation was incorporated on April 30, 1953 and grew fast between the 1970s and the 1990s as the city annexed land to the west. That means a lot of houses and condo buildings with plumbing that is thirty to fifty years old. Galvanized supply lines, cast iron drains and original water heaters all show up in our calls.

Newer homes tend to have PEX and PVC, which bring a different set of problems, mostly poor connections and fittings. Larger lots, such as those in Plantation Acres, can have long runs between the meter and the house, so yard leaks are a regular call.

We read your house before we recommend anything. If a repair will hold, we do it. If the pipe is nearing the end, we say that plainly and explain repiping or sewer line repair as options.

How an emergency call works

  1. You call (954) 697-4920 and tell us what is happening and where.
  2. We tell you what to shut off and whether it is safe to stay in the house.
  3. We send a plumber and give you an arrival window.
  4. The plumber finds the cause, explains the options and gives you a price before starting.
  5. We repair the problem, test it and tell you what to watch for.

If water is spreading, close the main valve first. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 from outside before you call anyone else.

Water, sewer and permits in Plantation

Plantation Utilities pumps raw groundwater from the Biscayne aquifer and treats it with membrane softening at two plants. The city's 2020 water quality report says the disinfectant is chlorine with chloramines and does not list a hardness figure, so test your tap if scale is an issue. On the sewer side, the city lists 197 miles of gravity sewer main and 125 lift stations.

For water heater work, Plantation offers a same-day permit program for existing single-family homes, and the contractor has to be registered in the city system. We handle that step in our water heater replacement jobs.
