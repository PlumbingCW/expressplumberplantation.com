Storm season and plumbing in Lauderhill

Hurricane season runs June through November, and heavy rain stresses drains and sewer systems. After a big storm, backups and overflowing floor drains are common. Power outages can also affect water pressure and sump equipment, and surges damage electric water heaters.

Before the season, check that you know where your main shutoff is and that cleanouts are accessible. Our hydro jetting service clears lines before they fail.

Old pipes, new problems

Cast iron drains scale up and crack. Galvanized supply lines corrode and cut flow. In buildings with those materials, a clog may be a symptom of pipe that is closing in. We use a camera inspection to see what is happening, and for supply lines we explain when repiping is worth considering.

When a pipe bursts, our burst pipe repair crews stop the water and replace the failed section.

Before we arrive: what to do while you wait

Stop the water at the main

Turn the main valve fully clockwise. If you cannot find it, close the shutoff behind the nearest toilet and call us for the rest.

Switch off affected circuits

Flip breakers for wet rooms. Do not stand in water to reach a panel.

Contain the spread

Towels at door thresholds and a bucket under drips buy time, and moving valuables off the floor matters more than mopping.

Water heaters and plumbing in older buildings

Original water heaters in older Lauderhill buildings often live in closets with little clearance. We work in tight spaces, protect floors and make sure the relief line is routed safely. Where a unit sits in a shared hallway closet, we coordinate with the building.

If your heater is old, the data plate shows its age. We read it with you and say honestly whether it is worth repair or time for replacement. Our replacement crews handle the permit as well.
