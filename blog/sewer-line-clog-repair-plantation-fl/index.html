Sewer Line Clog Repair: A Plantation, FL Homeowner Guide

When several drains slow down at once, the clog sits in the main line. Here is how to respond and what a plumber does.

Call (954) 697-4920See all services

By the Plantation Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-11-09 · Updated 2026-11-09

A sewer line clog is different from a sink clog. It affects the whole house. Toilets gurgle, tubs fill when the washing machine drains and the lowest fixture overflows first.

If that sounds familiar, the steps below keep the damage small until help arrives. Our sewer line cleaning crews take it from there.

How to recognize a main line clog

  • More than one fixture drains slowly at the same time.
  • A toilet gurgles when a sink or shower runs.
  • Water backs up in a tub or floor drain on the lowest level.
  • A sewer smell inside or near the cleanout.
  • Water rises in a fixture when the washing machine drains.

First steps to protect your home

Stop using water. Do not flush, shower or run laundry. Pour no chemicals down the drain. If water is coming up, move belongings away and keep children and pets out of the area. Take photos for insurance, and call us for dispatch.

Locate the cleanout

The cleanout is a capped pipe, often three or four inches wide, outside the house or in the garage. Do not open it unless you know what to expect. Pressure from a blocked line can push wastewater out when the cap comes off. We open it safely and run the equipment.

How plumbers clear the line

  1. We open the cleanout and check the line.
  2. We feed a cable machine through to break the blockage.
  3. For roots or grease, we use hydro jetting to scour the pipe.
  4. We camera the line to find any damage behind the clog.
  5. We explain whether you need only cleaning or also a repair.

Why it might come back

Roots, a belly in the pipe, a broken joint or years of grease can make the same clog return. If it does, the answer is an inspection and a repair, not another cleaning. The sewer camera inspection shows what is wrong.

What the cleanout tells us

When we open the cleanout, we learn a lot. If water flows out or the pipe is full and standing, the clog sits downstream. If the cleanout is dry, the blockage is between the house and the cleanout. That narrows the search and speeds the clearing.

A clear cleanout with a backup inside the house points to a different problem, such as a blockage in a branch line.

Do not use chemical drain openers on a main line

Chemical cleaners do not reach a distant clog and may sit in the pipe. They can burn skin and damage the plumber's equipment. If you already used one, tell us before we open the pipe, because it changes the safety steps.

After the line is clear

  • Run water in every fixture to confirm everything drains.
  • Ask for camera footage if the clog was severe.
  • Plan a cleaning schedule if roots or grease are the cause.
  • Consider a cleanout if your house lacks one.
  • Call early if symptoms return.

Talking to your landlord, board or insurer

If you rent, call your landlord or manager and give them the plumber's name and number. If you live in a condo, tell the board right away, because a stack clog can affect other units. If the backup damaged floors or belongings, photograph everything and contact your insurer before cleanup. Our reports and footage give you something concrete to share.

Making a list for the plumber

  • Which fixtures are slow and which are fine.
  • When it started and whether it has happened before.
  • Whether it got worse after rain or heavy use.
  • What you have already tried.
  • Where the cleanout is, if you know.

With that list, dispatch can send the right tools. It also helps if the problem turns out to be in the city main, since you can describe the timeline to the utility.

When to call right away

Do not wait if sewage is rising, if you have only one bathroom, if the smell is strong or if water is reaching flooring. Those cases are emergencies, and our dispatch line is staffed at every hour. If the problem is a slow drain with nothing rising, you can schedule a visit for the next open day.

Cleanup after a backup

Do not try to dry a soaked area with a household vacuum, because sewage can foul the motor. Wastewater carries bacteria, so wear gloves and boots and ventilate. Remove porous materials that soaked up sewage, such as carpet and padding. Hard floors can be washed and disinfected.

A restoration company can handle larger spills, and we can tell you when the line is clear so they can start. Keep children and pets out until the area has been cleaned and has dried.

Codes for cleanouts and repairs

Florida follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). If a cleanout is missing, adding one must meet that code.

Preventing the next clog

  • Keep grease out of the kitchen sink.
  • Flush only toilet paper and waste.
  • Avoid planting large trees near the sewer line.
  • Clean the line on a schedule if roots are a known problem.

Plantation's sewer network and your cleanout
