Main Sewer Line Repair Cost in Plantation, FL

No honest plumber can name a sewer repair price without seeing inside the pipe. This guide shows what moves the number and how to compare bids.

Call (954) 697-4920See all services

By the Plantation Express Plumbing team · Published 2026-10-06 · Updated 2026-10-06

Sewer repair prices vary because the jobs vary. One home needs a short patch in a front lawn. Another needs a long stretch replaced under a driveway and a mature tree. The pipe can be the same age and the bills very different.

Rather than quote a figure that will mislead you, this guide lists the factors that matter, in the order they usually matter. For the repair work itself, see our sewer line repair page.

Step one: the camera, not a guess

Every fair quote starts with a camera inspection. The footage shows whether the problem is a blockage, a crack, a root intrusion, a sagging section or a collapse. It also shows how far along the line the damage sits.

A plumber who quotes a full replacement without a camera is guessing. Ask to see the footage and get the distance from the cleanout to each defect. Our sewer camera inspection service gives you that.

The factors that move the price

Depth of the pipe

Deeper pipe means bigger excavation, shoring and more soil to move. A line that sits a couple of feet down is much easier than one far below the surface.

Length of the damaged section

A single break costs less than a long stretch of failed pipe. Replacement of the whole run from house to street takes the most material and labor.

What lies above the pipe

Digging through grass is simpler than cutting concrete, breaking up a driveway or working around a tree. Restoration of the surface is part of the price.

Method

Spot repair, pipe lining and open-trench replacement carry different costs. Lining can avoid heavy digging but needs a pipe that is still mostly intact.

Access and permits

Tight access, shared fences and utility crossings slow the work. Some jobs need permits, which add steps.

Comparing repair methods

Questions to ask every contractor

  • Did you inspect the line with a camera, and can I see the footage?
  • Which method are you proposing and why?
  • What surface restoration is included?
  • Will you pull the permit and schedule the inspection?
  • What happens if you find something different once you dig?
  • Is the price written, and what would change it?

Who pays, you or the city

The city maintains the main in the street, and your house line is generally the owner's concern, though the dividing point depends on the property. The camera footage shows where the problem sits. If it is on the city side, report it to the utility. If it is on your side, you will need a plumber.

Red flags in a sewer repair quote

  • A price for full replacement with no camera footage.
  • No mention of permits or inspection.
  • A very low bid that leaves out surface restoration.
  • Pressure to sign the same day.
  • No written scope or warranty terms.

Why two honest quotes can differ

One contractor may propose lining where another proposes digging, and the second may include a new cleanout and the first may not. One may price concrete restoration, while another leaves it to you. Ask each to list what is included. The honest approach is to compare scopes first and prices second.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Find the cleanout and clear the area around it. Note when the problems began and which fixtures are affected. Gather any past repair records. If you have a recent camera video from a home inspection, keep it. The more information we have, the faster we diagnose.

Payment, scheduling and the order of work

Ask how payment is structured and when the final payment is due. Ask when the permit is pulled and how inspection fits the schedule. A good contractor tells you the order of events: locate utilities, excavate, repair, test, backfill, restore the surface and inspect.

Plan for the sewer to be out of service for part of a day, so arrange alternatives for bathing and laundry.

Preventing a second repair

  • Keep grease and wipes out of the line.
  • Have the line cleaned if roots are known.
  • Install a cleanout if your house lacks one.
  • Keep large tree roots away from the line.
  • Camera the line every few years if the house is old.

Three homeowner scenarios, no dollar figures

Picture a short break in a front lawn near the street. It is one of the simpler jobs, because digging is small and restoration is sod. Picture a long section under a driveway with a mature tree nearby. That one costs far more because of concrete, access and roots. Picture a clogged old line that lining can restore. That sits between the two, depending on pipe condition.

These examples show why a flat price makes little sense, and why a camera is the first step.

Permits and codes behind the price

Florida follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC). Sewer replacement can require a permit and inspection, which is one reason quotes differ.

Insurance and records

Homeowners policies treat sewer line damage differently, and coverage depends on the cause and on any endorsements. Ask your insurer before work begins, and keep camera footage, invoices and photos. They help whichever way the claim goes.

Plantation's sewer system and your line

The City of Plantation's wastewater collection page lists 197 miles of gravity sewer mains, 57 miles of force mains and 125 lift stations. Your lateral connects to that network, so a blockage that comes back may sit in the city main instead of the house line.

Florida also has a statewide service called Sunshine 811 for utility locates before digging. Contractors in Davie, Sunrise and Plantation all use it, and it keeps your trench clear of buried lines.
