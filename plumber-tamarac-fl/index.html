Tamarac is a city of communities. Villas, condo buildings and active adult neighborhoods each have their own rules, shutoffs and boards, and that shapes how plumbing calls work.

We serve Tamarac from our Plantation base, and we plan every visit around the community and its access requirements.

Community by community

In a condo, the shutoff for your unit may be inside a closet, in a riser or on a floor above. Some communities require advance notice for contractors, and management may need to sign off on work that affects shared lines. We ask about those details on the first call, so we arrive ready.

In a villa or single-family neighborhood, the usual homeowner shutoffs apply, and we can usually start right away.

Water heaters and leaks in older buildings

Many Tamarac buildings are decades old, which means original water heaters in closets, copper pipes with pinholes and drain stacks that run through several units. A leak in one unit can drip into another, so speed matters.

Our water heater replacement crews handle swaps in tight closets, and our leak detection team finds the source before walls come open.

Before we arrive: what to do while you wait

Locate your shutoff

In a villa or condo the valve may be inside a closet or at a riser. Turn it clockwise until it stops, and ask your board where shared valves are.

Turn off the power

Cut breakers to the wet area and avoid any outlet that is damp or dripping.

Protect the floors

Towels and a wet vacuum slow the spread. Move furniture away from the wall where water is coming through.

Communities with shared plumbing

Condo communities often share drain stacks and supply risers. When one unit has a clog, the stack can back up into others below. We trace the blockage, clear it and report what we found to the owner or the board. If a stack needs repair, we explain the scope so the association can plan.

For villas, the plumbing is usually separate, and we can often fix the problem within a single visit.

What we need to know before arriving

  • The community or building name and the unit number.
  • Gate or access instructions and who will let us in.
  • Whether the association requires notice or a certificate of insurance for contractors.
  • Whether a neighbor below you is affected.
  • What you have already shut off.

Working with associations

If your community has a management office, tell them about the problem and ask for the contractor approval process. We can provide a written description of what we find to help. For common-area problems, the association decides who authorizes work, and we coordinate with the person you name.
